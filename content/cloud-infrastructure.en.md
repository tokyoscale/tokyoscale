Private compute is sometimes a better fit than public-cloud metering alone. GPU workstations, CI workers, and agent hosts belong in a rack whose power and connectivity are known—and someone has to own monitoring, patching, and recovery. TokyoScale takes that from design through operations.

## Colocation

We arrange rack space, power, and connectivity in a Tokyo data center, with the facility operator. Line speed, power draw, distribution, on-site work, and contract terms are settled with the facility before a formal order. Initial circuit and power setup moves with the equipment install.

## Procurement and build

We select and procure the machines, then assemble, ship, rack, burn in, and acceptance-test them as one job.

- **GPU development workstations** — local training, inference trials, and model checks
- **CI and test workers** — builds and tests that stay on the rack
- **AI agent hosts** — small machines meant to stay up
- **Network** — router and VPN, switching, remote KVM, and the first in-rack cabling

Model numbers, PSU headroom, and whether a chassis actually fits the rack are checked before order. If something does not fit, we change the spec in writing.

## Security and recovery

The initial build includes security design, endpoint protection, multi-factor authentication, access rights, and hardening. We stand up backup and recovery, then run a restore drill. Design notes and runbooks stay with the system so a handover does not reset the procedure.

## Operations

Monitoring is not the end of the job. A typical year of operations includes:

- 24-hour automated monitoring
- Weekly inspection, including an on-site look
- Vulnerability and patch management
- First-line triage
- A monthly report
- Periodic security reviews

Data-center fees, equipment, and operations are quoted separately—they are different scopes. AI usage, endpoint-protection licenses, backup storage, dedicated circuits, and spare parts stay out of the quote unless we name them.

## Technical consulting

Platform design, performance checks, and acceptance criteria come before procurement. Before adding machines, we agree what the rack is for and who does what when it fails.
